product

## Users

Pabriq serves four distinct roles in Made-To-Order businesses:

- **Owner**: Business decision-maker. Needs revenue visibility, team oversight, and platform configuration. Full access to everything.
- **Admin / Operations Lead**: Day-to-day manager. Handles orders, products, customers, invoices, production config, and team members. The primary power user.
- **Operator / Production Worker**: Floor-level task executor. Sees only the production kanban. Advances tasks through workflow stages. Needs fast, clear task context (specs, files, quantity, customer).
- **Customer (External)**: Places orders via secure token link (no login required). Submits design specs, uploads files, tracks production progress, pays invoices.

Primary verticals: custom merchandise, printing, furniture, clothing, and any produce-to-order operation. Regional focus: Indonesian market (IDR, Bahasa Indonesia, Biteship shipping).

## Product Purpose

Pabriq manages the full lifecycle of custom production orders: customer intake, order approval, configurable production workflows with kanban boards, invoice generation, and delivery tracking. It replaces the spreadsheet-plus-WhatsApp chaos that most small MTO shops run on.

Success looks like: an operator can see exactly what to produce next, an admin can spot bottlenecks in seconds, and a customer can track their order without sending a WhatsApp message.

## Brand Personality

Confident, Clean, Practical.

Pabriq is a working tool, not a showpiece. It should feel like opening a well-organized workshop: everything in its place, nothing fancy, but you trust it completely. The voice is direct and efficient. No playful mascots, no startup buzzwords, no marketing fluff. Just clear information, fast actions, and zero ambiguity.

Confidence comes from precision, not decoration. Clean means uncluttered, not sterile. Practical means every pixel earns its place.

## Anti-references

- Not enterprise-ugly: no soul-crushing gray admin panels, no nested tables-within-tables, no "enterprise dashboard" layouts with 47 cards of equal importance.
- Not consumer-playful: no illustrations of happy people, no rounded-everything toy aesthetics, no bright multi-color gradients for decoration.
- Not SaaS-landing-page: no hero sections with floating mockups, no "10x your productivity" claims, no fake social proof bars.
- Not Notion-cluttered: not every feature needs to be a slash command or toggle. Some things should just be a form field.
- Not generic-AI-tool: no glassmorphism, no purple-neon-on-dark, no "AI-powered" branding. This is a production management tool, not a chatbot.

## Design Principles

1. **Information density with clarity**: Show what matters without overwhelming. A kanban board should communicate status at a glance, not require a click-through to understand.
2. **Context over navigation**: The customer portal shows production progress directly. The operator sees specs and files on the task card. Reduce "go somewhere else to find out" moments.
3. **Role-appropriate views**: Owner sees revenue, operator sees tasks, customer sees progress. Same data, different lenses. Never show someone something they can't act on.
4. **Fast actions, zero ambiguity**: Every button should do exactly what it says. Status transitions are explicit. No "are you sure?" modals for routine operations.
5. **Multi-tenant isolation by default**: Every query is org-scoped. Every view is filtered. Security is structural, not bolted on.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline: 4.5:1 contrast ratios on text, keyboard navigation on all interactive elements, focus-visible indicators, semantic HTML structure. Bahasa Indonesia as the primary UI language alongside English. Mobile-responsive for operators on the shop floor.
